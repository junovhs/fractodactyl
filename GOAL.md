# Standing goal: discover useful computational shortcuts

**This is the project's durable objective, not an assignment to one technique or industry.**

Discover, test, and eventually generalize mathematical methods that make previously expensive **scientific or industrial computation more efficiently**, especially where nonlinear dynamics contain exploitable structure. Fractodactyl supplied the first techniques and research discipline; rendering is **not** the goal.

The expected output is **findings**: credible new methods, repeatable incremental improvements, transferable gains, useful failures, limits of known methods, and unexpected connections. A negative test that eliminates a plausible approach is progress. Avoid novelty claims without prior-art review.

**Mandatory research distinction:** Established mathematics with a new implementation benchmark is **not** a newly discovered algorithm. Before any new experiment, agents must actually search the published literature, **commit the literal search queries, dated results with source links, closest predecessor and exact unresolved gap** alongside the new hypothesis, and classify known-method benchmarking versus an open scientific question. [PRIOR_ART.md](PRIOR_ART.md) defines the enforceable audit and blocker. When searches cannot run, the new experiment is **BLOCKED**; no source-free novelty narrative is allowed. Preserve incremental gains without inventing mathematical originality.

## Agent autonomy

Agents are **not required to follow a preselected technical roadmap** or keep pursuing power electronics. At each research session, independently read the evidence, propose competing leads across domains, select the **highest information-to-cost** test, run it, and update the [frontier](FRONTIER.md). The owner need not know which algorithm to pursue. Follow evidence rather than fashion, hunches, or sunk cost.

Search broadly enough to find surprising connections, but demand a concrete mechanism, comparable baseline, and cheap falsifiable probe before investing. Preserve cross-domain transfer opportunities; avoid both aimless brainstorming and premature commitment to one application.

## How we recognize a valuable discovery

- **Usefulness:** a plausible real workload (science, industry, computation), not merely a pretty demonstration.
- **Transfer:** a mechanism that plausibly works beyond a handcrafted fractal coordinate or single toy model.
- **Truthfulness:** independent accuracy checks first, rigorous guarantees where needed; no overstated certification.
- **Economics:** **no mandatory speedup floor—not 3×, not 1.10×.** A reliable 1% gain can be meaningful in a heavily used computation; a 10%, 30%, or 2× gain deserves to be preserved. Measure a realistic workload against the strongest accurate same-math/specialist rival, with startup, warm reuse, amortization and absolute time saved clearly distinguished. Allocate further research effort by expected cumulative saved compute, applicability, uncertainty and implementation cost; treat 3× as an ambitious breakthrough tier, never a universal survival gate.
- **Autonomy:** hypotheses and next experiments are selected from evidence; all tests and dead ends are recorded for other agents.

**Two independent questions:** (1) Is a gain accurate, repeatable and fairly measured? (2) Does its expected real-world payoff justify more investigation? Do not erase a genuine positive effect merely because it misses a predeclared experimental speed target; preserve the original `GATE: FAIL` and record the narrower opportunity for a new preregistered test. Likewise, a small gain may be real but not a priority to optimize further. Existing P-card numerical thresholds remain historical facts, not defaults for new work.

A general-purpose mathematical compiler is a **research aspiration, not an assumed feasible outcome**. We may discover narrower valuable algorithms instead.
