Holomorphic Renormalization and Local Return Maps for Accelerated Orbit Compression Near Mandelbrot MinibrotsTheoretical Foundations of Local Return Maps and RenormalizationThe iteration of the complex quadratic family $f_c(z) = z^2 + c$ exhibits profound multi-scale self-similarity driven by holomorphic renormalization. For parameter values $c$ located deep within the Mandelbrot set $\mathbb{M}$ near a period-$p$ miniature Mandelbrot set (minibrot), the dynamics of the $p$-th iterate $f_c^p(z)$ locally mimic the global dynamics of $f_c(z)$ on a smaller spatial scale. The foundational framework established by Adrien Douady and John H. Hubbard formalizes this self-similarity through the theory of polynomial-like maps.A polynomial-like map of degree $d$ is a triple $(f, U, V)$, where $U$ and $V$ are open topological disks in $\mathbb{C}$ such that $U$ is compactly contained in $V$ ($U \Subset V$), and $f: U \to V$ is a proper holomorphic mapping of degree $d$. Douady and Hubbard’s Straightening Theorem asserts that any quadratic-like map ($d=2$) is hybrid conjugate to a unique quadratic polynomial $P_{\tilde{c}}(z) = z^2 + \tilde{c}$ for some $\tilde{c} \in \mathbb{M}$. The hybrid equivalence preserves the conformal dynamics while smoothing out non-conformal deformations outside the filled Julia set. Under this conjugacy $h$, the local return map satisfies $h \circ f_c^p \circ h^{-1}(z) = P_{\tilde{c}}(z)$, establishing an analytical equivalence between the local return dynamics across a period-$p$ cycle and the full quadratic family.When evaluating orbits near a period-$p$ minibrot center $c_0$, the exact local return map is given by the $p$-th composition of the quadratic polynomial, $f_c^p(z) = (f_c \circ f_c \circ \dots \circ f_c)(z)$. The algebraic degree of $f_c^p(z)$ as a polynomial in $z$ is $2^p$. For high periods (for example, $p = 10^3$ to $p = 10^6$), evaluating $f_c^p(z)$ directly as a degree-$2^p$ polynomial is computationally impossible. However, within a sufficiently small neighborhood of a periodic orbit, $f_c^p(z)$ can be expanded as a local power series in terms of perturbations $\delta z = z - Z$ and $\delta c = c - C$, where $Z$ is a high-precision reference orbit point and $C$ is the reference parameter center:$$z_{n+p} = F(z_n, \delta c) = Z_{n+p} + \sum_{j=1}^{d_z} \sum_{k=0}^{d_c} C_{j,k} (\delta z_n)^j (\delta c)^k + \mathcal{O}(\vert{}\delta z_n\vert{}^{d_z+1} + \vert{}\delta c|^{d_c+1})$$The effective degree $d_z$ of the truncated return map $F(z_n, \delta c)$ determines its accuracy and domain of validity. Truncating at $d_z = 1$ yields a Bivariate Linear Approximation (BLA), which captures local linear stretching and rotation. Truncating at higher degrees ($d_z \ge 2$) yields high-order Series Approximations (SA) or biseries maps capable of capturing quadratic foldings and higher-order geometric distortions.Near parabolic periodic points—where the multiplier $\Lambda = \frac{d}{dz} f_{c_0}^p(z_0)$ approaches a root of unity $e^{2\pi i p/q}$—standard polynomial Taylor expansions fail due to slow convergence along Fatou corridors. In these regions, parabolic and near-parabolic renormalization utilize Fatou coordinates ($\Phi_{\text{att}}, \Phi_{\text{rep}}$) that map croissant-shaped fundamental regions $S_{\text{rep}}$ into transit cylinders $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}$. The corresponding return map is represented by a horn map $E_f = \Phi_{\text{att}} \circ \Phi_{\text{rep}}^{-1}$, which translates orbit trajectories through parabolic channels with controlled non-linearity.Geometric Scaling Laws and Validity DomainsThe spatial dimensions of minibrots and the domain over which a local return map remains accurate are governed by the derivative properties of the reference orbit. Let $c_0$ be the nucleus (center) of a period-$p$ hyperbolic component, where $f_{c_0}^p(0) = 0$. The multiplier of the period-$p$ cycle is given by $\Lambda = \left. \frac{\partial}{\partial z} f_{c_0}^p(z) \right\vert{}_{z=z_0} = \prod_{i=0}^{p-1} 2 z_i$. For a superattracting center $c_0$, the multiplier at the critical origin vanishes ($\Lambda = 0$). However, the derivative with respect to the parameter $c$, given by $B_p = \left. \frac{\partial}{\partial c} f_c^p(0) \right\vert{}_{c=c_0}$, governs the local parameter scaling. The effective spatial diameter $r$ of the main pseudocardioid of a period-$p$ minibrot scales inverse-exponentially with respect to the period $p$ and the accumulation of local derivatives:$$r \approx \frac{1}{\beta \vert{}\Lambda\vert{}^{p/2}} \quad \text{or} \quad r \approx \left\vert{} \frac{1}{\left. \frac{\partial}{\partial c} f_c^p(z_0) \right\vert{}_{c=c_0}} \right\vert{}$$where $\beta$ is a geometric distortion factor determined by the internal address and satellite waking structure. If $c_0$ is surrounded by satellite minibrots, a satellite at relative position $c'$ with diameter $r'$ exhibits a scaling behavior of $r' \approx r^2$, reflecting quadratic parameter folding near sub-component roots.The domain of validity for a compressed local map $z_{n+k} \approx F(z_n, \delta c)$ depends on the allowable truncation error relative to the working numerical precision $\epsilon$ (for instance, $\epsilon = 2^{-24}$ for single precision or $\epsilon = 2^{-53}$ for double precision). For a single-step linear approximation where $\Delta z_{n+1} = 2 Z_n \Delta z_n + \Delta c$, the omitted non-linear term is $(\Delta z_n)^2$. Requiring the non-linear term to be bounded by $\epsilon$ times the magnitude of the dominant linear term gives $\vert{}\Delta z_n\vert{}^2 \le \epsilon \vert{}2 Z_n \Delta z_n + \Delta c\vert{}$. Assuming the parameter perturbation term $\Delta c$ is small relative to the state perturbation, factoring out $\vert{}\Delta z_n\vert{}$ yields the validity domain radius:$$R_n \approx \frac{\epsilon \vert{}2 Z_n\vert{} - \vert{}B_{n,1}\vert{} \vert{}\Delta c\vert{}_{\max}}{\vert{}A_{n,1}\vert{}} \approx \epsilon \vert{}Z_n\vert{}$$This relationship establishes that the validity domain radius $R_n$ scales linearly with the magnitude of the reference orbit point $\vert{}Z_n\vert{}$. When the reference orbit passes close to the critical point ($Z_n \approx 0$), the linear validity domain collapses ($R_n \to 0$), forcing the algorithm to fallback to non-linear perturbation steps. Across a full minibrot return period $p$, the composite validity domain radius $R_{\text{comp}}$ is bounded by the minimum scaled derivative encountered along the orbit, scaling as $R_{\text{comp}} \sim \mathcal{O}(\vert{}\Lambda\vert{}^{-1})$. Finding a period-$p$ hyperbolic nucleus requires solving the equation $f_c^p(0) = 0$ or finding a periodic point $w$ such that $f_c^p(w) - w = 0$. Newton’s method in one complex variable updates the estimate $w_k$ via:$$w_{k+1} = w_k - \frac{f_c^p(w_k) - w_k}{\frac{\partial}{\partial z} f_c^p(w_k) - 1}$$where $\frac{\partial}{\partial z} f_c^p(w_k)$ is evaluated using the chain rule $\prod_{i=0}^{p-1} 2 z_i$. To locate boundary points or internal components with a specific internal multiplier $b = r e^{2\pi i \theta}$ ($\vert{}r\vert{} \le 1$), a two-dimensional complex Newton-Raphson scheme solves the system $F_p(z, c) - z = 0$ and $\frac{\partial}{\partial z} F_p(z, c) - b = 0$. The Jacobian matrix for this system requires computing second derivatives $\frac{\partial^2 F_p}{\partial z^2}$ and mixed derivatives $\frac{\partial^2 F_p}{\partial z \partial c}$, accumulated iteratively alongside the primary reference orbit.The most computationally efficient local return map architecture is Bivariate Linear Approximation (BLA). A BLA represents a skip of $l$ iterations starting from reference index $n$ as a linear map: $\Delta z_{n+l} = A_{n,l} \Delta z_n + B_{n,l} \Delta c$, where $A_{n,l}$ and $B_{n,l}$ are complex scalar coefficients, accompanied by a validity radius $R_{n,l}$. For a single iteration step ($l=1$), the recurrence $\Delta z_{n+1} = 2 Z_n \Delta z_n + (\Delta z_n)^2 + \Delta c$ establishes the initial coefficients $A_{n,1} = 2 Z_n$ and $B_{n,1} = 1$, with validity radius $R_{n,1} = \max\left(0, \frac{\epsilon \vert{}Z_n\vert{} - \vert{}B_{n,1}\vert{} \vert{}\Delta c\vert{}_{\max}}{\vert{}A_{n,1}\vert{}}\right)$, where $\vert{}\Delta c\vert{}_{\max}$ is the maximum parameter radius across the target rendering viewport.To construct long-range return maps across thousands or millions of iterations efficiently, adjacent BLAs are combined using a binary tree merging algorithm. Given two adjacent BLAs, $x$ (skipping $l_x$ steps from $n$) and $y$ (skipping $l_y$ steps from $n+l_x$), their composition $z = y \circ x$ yields $\Delta z_{n+l_x+l_y} = A_y (A_x \Delta z_n + B_x \Delta c) + B_y \Delta c = (A_y A_x) \Delta z_n + (A_y B_x + B_y) \Delta c$. The merged coefficients and composite validity radius $R_z$ are computed as:$$A_z = A_y A_x$$$$B_z = A_y B_x + B_y$$$$R_z = \max\left(0, \, \min\left(R_x, \, \frac{R_y - \vert{}B_x\vert{} \vert{}\Delta c\vert{}_{\max}}{\vert{}A_x\vert{}}\right)\right)$$Starting with $M$ single-step BLAs across a reference orbit of length $M$, adjacent non-overlapping pairs are merged to form $\lceil M/2 \rceil$ two-step BLAs. This composition is repeated hierarchically for $\log_2 M$ levels, producing a complete binary tree of BLAs in $O(M)$ total time and $O(M)$ memory space. Tree construction begins at iteration index 1 rather than 0 because iteration 0 originates at the critical point $Z_0 = 0$, where $A_{0,1} = 0$ yields a zero validity radius. Memory efficiency is further maximized during rendering by employing linear arena allocators to store tree nodes sequentially in pre-allocated buffers.During pixel iteration, the execution logic evaluates the BLA tree at the current iteration index $m$. The search tests the largest available skip interval $L$ at index $m$. If the perturbation magnitude satisfies $\vert{}\Delta z\vert{} < R_{m,L}$, the BLA application advances the orbit state by $2^L$ iterations in a single step: $\Delta z \leftarrow A_{m,L} \Delta z + B_{m,L} \Delta c$ and $m \leftarrow m + 2^L$. If the validity check fails, the search recurses down the tree hierarchy to evaluate smaller sub-interval skips. If no BLA is valid at index $m$, the system falls back to a standard non-linear perturbation step: $\Delta z \leftarrow 2 Z_m \Delta z + \Delta z^2 + \Delta c$ and $m \leftarrow m + 1$.When higher accuracy is required over wider local domains, high-order polynomial expansions replace BLA. Series Approximation (SA) expands $\Delta z_n$ as a multi-variable Taylor series: $\Delta z_n = \sum_{j=1}^{d_z} A_j^{(n)} (\Delta z_0)^j + \Delta c \sum_{k=0}^{d_c} B_k^{(n)} (\Delta z_0)^k + \mathcal{O}(\vert{} \Delta z_0 \vert{}^{d_z+1})$, where the series coefficients $A_j^{(n)}$ and $B_k^{(n)}$ are precomputed sequentially along the reference orbit. For points trapped near deep nested minibrots, the NanoMB1 (biseries) algorithm compresses period steps $p$ by forming a double series in $\Delta z$ and $\Delta c$. The return map over a full period $p$ is evaluated repeatedly as $\Delta z_{k+1} = F_p(\Delta z_k, \Delta c)$ until $\vert{}\Delta z_k\vert{}$ exceeds a pre-calculated escape radius $R_{\text{esc}}$. Once $R_{\text{esc}}$ is exceeded, the orbit escapes the local minibrot domain and transitions back to standard perturbation or to an outer minibrot in a nested chain (NanoMB2).Rigorous Certification via Ball ArithmeticTo elevate local return map approximations from heuristic numerical speedups to mathematically certified enclosures, operations must be conducted within arbitrary-precision interval or ball arithmetic. The C library Arb (integrated into FLINT) provides an optimized implementation of complex ball arithmetic (acb_t). A complex ball $z = [m \pm r] \in \mathbb{C}$ is represented by a floating-point midpoint $m \in \mathbb{C}$ and a rigorous upper-bound radius $r \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ that encapsulates all rounding errors, initial parameter uncertainties, and truncation residuals.When evaluating a truncated return map polynomial $P_N(\Delta z) = \sum_{k=0}^N c_k (\Delta z)^k$, certification requires establishing an explicit bound on the tail residual $E_N(\Delta z) = \sum_{k=N+1}^\infty c_k (\Delta z)^k$. If $F(z)$ is analytic on a closed disk $\overline{D}(0, R_{\text{dom}})$, Cauchy’s Estimate bounds the polynomial coefficients: $\vert{}c_k\vert{} \le \frac{M(R_{\text{dom}})}{R_{\text{dom}}^k}$, where $M(R_{\text{dom}}) = \max_{\vert{}z\vert{} = R_{\text{dom}}} \vert{}F(z)\vert{}$. For any input ball $\Delta z$ with $\vert{}\Delta z\vert{} \le \rho < R_{\text{dom}}$, the truncation error $E_N(\Delta z)$ is rigorously bounded by a geometric series sum:$$\vert{}E_N(\Delta z)\vert{} \le \sum_{k=N+1}^\infty \frac{M(R_{\text{dom}})}{R_{\text{dom}}^k} \rho^k = M(R_{\text{dom}}) \frac{(\rho / R_{\text{dom}})^{N+1}}{1 - (\rho / R_{\text{dom}})}$$In Arb, polynomial evaluation is executed using acb_poly_evaluate, which tracks interval widths using Horner’s scheme or fast Taylor shift algorithms. The rigorous tail bound $\vert{}E_N(\Delta z)\vert{}$ is directly added to the radius component $r$ of the resulting output ball.When chaining $K$ BLA steps using ball arithmetic, floating-point rounding errors and non-linear truncation bounds are certified at each node. Let the input state be encapsulated by ball $[\Delta z_n \pm r_n]$ and parameter delta by $[\Delta c \pm r_c]$. The certified BLA transform yields $\Delta z_{n+l} = A_{n,l} \Delta z_n + B_{n,l} \Delta c$, with the output error radius bounded by:$$r_{n+l} = \vert{}A_{n,l}\vert{} r_n + \vert{}B_{n,l}\vert{} r_c + \epsilon_{\text{round}} (\vert{}A_{n,l} \Delta z_n\vert{} + \vert{}B_{n,l} \Delta c\vert{}) + \delta_{\text{trunc}}$$where $\delta_{\text{trunc}}$ represents the maximum possible contribution of the omitted squared term $\sum_{i=0}^{l-1} \vert{}A_{n,l-1-i}\vert{}^2 (\Delta z_{n+i})^2$, computed via rigorous upper-bound interval arithmetic. If at any iteration the radius component $r_{n+l}$ exceeds a predefined tolerance, the certification framework rejects the skip step and forces a precision expansion or interval subdivision.Comprehensive Method Comparison, Implementation Mechanics, and Failure AnalysisThe operational characteristics, computational complexity, and failure mechanisms of local return maps vary significantly across different approximation paradigms. A glitch occurs when the perturbed variable $z_n$ becomes comparable in magnitude to the reference orbit $Z_m$, but opposite in sign, resulting in catastrophic loss of precision during the addition $Z_m + z_n$. Glitches are detected numerically using the condition $\vert{}Z_m + z_n\vert{} < \vert{}z_n\vert{}$ or pattern matching. When this condition is triggered, nearby pixel orbits lose relative resolution and create unrendered visual artifacts.This failure is resolved through orbit rebasing:Re-center the reference orbit by setting the new reference value to $Z_m' = Z_m + z_n$.Reset the perturbation variable $z_n \leftarrow 0$ and reference index $m \leftarrow 0$.Re-initialize the BLA tree or series approximation coefficients relative to the new reference orbit $Z_m'$.To handle dense glitch regions efficiently, modern renderers maintain multiple reference orbits starting from distinct initial conditions or critical points in parallel, dynamically selecting the orbit $o$ that minimizes $\vert{}(Z - Z_o) + z\vert{}$.When the reference orbit passes near the critical point ($Z_m \approx 0$), the derivative vanishes ($\frac{\partial}{\partial z} f(Z_m) = 0$). As a result, the validity domain radius $R_{m,1} = \epsilon \vert{}2 Z_m\vert{}$ collapses to zero, rendering all linear approximations starting at iteration $m$ invalid. Under critical breakdown, the iteration loop fails all BLA validity tests at index $m$ and falls back to standard non-linear perturbation steps ($\Delta z_{m+1} = 2 Z_m \Delta z_m + \Delta z_m^2 + \Delta c$). Multi-reference switching resolves this by dynamically transitioning the reference orbit to an alternative critical point orbit where $Z_m \neq 0$.For non-complex-analytic fractals—such as the Burning Ship fractal ($f(z) = (\vert{}\text{Re}(z)\vert{} + i \vert{}\text{Im}(z)\vert{})^2 + c$) or the Tricorn/Mandelbar ($f(z) = (\bar{z})^2 + c$)—the complex derivative $\frac{df}{dz}$ does not exist due to non-differentiable orientation reversals. To apply local return map compression to non-analytic formulas, scalar complex coefficients $A, B \in \mathbb{C}$ must be replaced with $2 \times 2$ real Jacobian matrices $J = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial \text{Re}(z_{n+1})}{\partial \text{Re}(z_n)} & \frac{\partial \text{Re}(z_{n+1})}{\partial \text{Im}(z_n)} \\ \frac{\partial \text{Im}(z_{n+1})}{\partial \text{Re}(z_n)} & \frac{\partial \text{Im}(z_{n+1})}{\partial \text{Im}(z_n)} \end{bmatrix}$. Single-step validity bounds are evaluated using singular value matrix norms ($\sigma_{\max}, \sigma_{\min}$) rather than complex absolute values: $R_n = \frac{\epsilon \cdot \sigma_{\min}(A) - \sigma_{\max}(B) \vert{}\Delta c\vert{}}{\sigma_{\max}(A)}$. While matrix-vector multiplication doubles the arithmetic operation count per step, it preserves orbit compression capabilities over deep non-analytic minibrot locations.ConclusionsLocal return map compression provides an algorithmic framework for accelerating deep-zoom evaluations near high-period minibrots in the Mandelbrot set.
